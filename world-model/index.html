One rigid rotation between open-vocabulary English and robot control

Most vision-language-action (VLA) models train a language backbone and robot action head jointly end-to-end. Here, language and robot control are trained completely separately and never share gradients, weights, or labels:

  • Pretrained English encoder (gte-small): an off-the-shelf Transformer sentence encoder running client-side in int8 on WebGPU/WebGL. It already knows real open-vocabulary English—concepts like “pump your hand like fists”, “yeet”, “do a 360”, “windshield wiper”, “boop”—and has never seen a robot joint angle, camera frame, or action.
  • Language-free compositional robot manifold & policy (4.37M params): an unsupervised execution-dynamics manifold over 26,003 unlabeled UR5e trajectories densely spanning factorized physical axes (height × lateral × gripper [open/closed/pulsing] × 10 motion axes × speed plus 6 cube-interaction primitives—zero text and zero skill labels), paired with a recurrent policy π(at | obst, zbeh) (GRU + 3 FiLM SwiGLU ResBlocks, 33D foveated visual obs + language-free DAgger). At runtime, the policy sees the robot's 6 joint encoders plus the red cube's 3D position read purely from the 224×224 front camera by frozen ViT-tiny + Readout2 (zero privileged cube state).
  • One single orthogonal rotation matrix R (zero-leakage protocol): given N paired (neutral physical description, robot clip) examples, we solve orthogonal Procrustes (UΣVᵀ = AᵀG, R = UVᵀ, satisfying RRᵀ = I to ~10⁻⁸). An automated assert bans 80+ user/metaphor test words (fist, pump, punch, throw, yeet, toss, fling, spin, 360, rotate around your base, windshield, wiper, wave, hi, bow, nod, clap, claw, wiggle, boop, dance, party, point, ceiling, sky) from every training caption—so prompts like “pump your hand like fists” and “slowly circle down low with your claw closed” are true zero-shot compositional generalizations through R.

Why a factorized motor manifold matters

Earlier tabletop-only controllers clamped the UR5e end-effector inside a tiny 35×70 cm box and only accepted a static cube coordinate. Even a discrete 17-skill controller fails on novel combinations like “pump your hand like fists” if no trajectory combines closed gripper + high elevation + vertical oscillation. Removing the invisible cage walls, switching to native 7D joint-space deltas (Δq0..5 + Robotiq gripper at 20 Hz), and densely covering factorized physical axes lets one rigid orthogonal rotation R align independent semantic dimensions (elevation, gripper state, motion axis, tempo, and cube physics) without scripting user prompts. You can also drag the red cube directly or click anywhere on the table to generate a placement command.

Limits

  • Zero-leakage social idioms: because all 80+ metaphor and social greeting words (like “say hi” and “wave”) are strictly banned from training captions, purely social prompts that contain no physical kinematics at all cannot be inferred from neutral motion descriptions alone. Phrasing with physical cues (like “swing your open hand side to side up high” or “pump your hand like fists”) maps cleanly onto the factorized manifold.
  • Repertoire boundary: while the English encoder embeds any sentence in English, the orthogonal rotation can only execute motions spanned by the robot's factorized 6-DOF trajectory manifold (elevation, lateral reach, gripper open/closed/pulsing, X/Y/Z oscillation, horizontal/vertical circles, base revolution, wrist pitch/roll, coupled sway, and 6 cube manipulation primitives).
  • Visual cube tracking range: cube coordinates come strictly from frozen ViT-tiny + Readout2 on the 224×224 front camera (0.51 cm median parity on the table). Launching the cube off the table automatically respawns a fresh cube when you run your next command (or click Reset).
  • Single rigid rotation vs. fine-tuning: constraining the cross-modal bridge to a single orthogonal matrix R keeps the geometry transparent and lets you break/restore alignment live with N, at the cost of higher placement scatter (~9 cm median) than a jointly fine-tuned VLA.
