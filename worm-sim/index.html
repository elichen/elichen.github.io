How it works

This page runs BAAIWorm (Zhao et al., Nature Computational Science 2024), a model of C. elegans that connects a detailed brain to a soft body in water. The original runs on a Linux workstation with NEURON and a CUDA viewer. Here both halves are new code, compiled to WebAssembly, and checked against the original's own data.

The brain

  • 136 neurons, each with its real 3D shape from the NeuroML connectome, cut into 23,848 compartments.
  • Each soma has up to 16 ion channels (Nicoletti et al. 2019): potassium, calcium and calcium-activated potassium channels, plus internal calcium.
  • 3,076 connections: 1,992 graded chemical synapses (651 excitatory, 1,341 inhibitory) and 1,084 gap junctions, placed on specific compartments.
  • The solver repeats NEURON's fixed-step method: backward Euler on each cell's tree, 5/3 ms steps, 600 steps per second of brain time.

Check: driven by the same input, it matches the motor-neuron voltages that NEURON recorded for BAAIWorm to within 0.0001 mV over 23.8 s, for all 80 motor neurons.

Brain to muscles

Every 100 ms, the mean voltage of 80 motor neurons goes through BAAIWorm's fitted 80 × 96 readout. The result is the activation of 96 muscles: 24 along each of the four muscle quadrants.

The body

  • A finite-element mesh with 984 vertices and 3,341 tetrahedra, solved with projective dynamics, the method of SoftCon (Min et al. 2019) that BAAIWorm uses.
  • Each muscle segment pulls through about 50 fiber constraints inside the tetrahedra. A muscle with activation a wants its fibers shortened to (1 − a) of their length.
  • Water pushes on each of the 1,466 surface triangles with SoftCon's drag and lift.
  • 8 steps of 1/240 s for every 100 ms of brain time, as in BAAIWorm.

Check: given the same muscle signals, no vertex of this body is more than 0.0000002 units from the same vertex in BAAIWorm's own body code during 20 s. (The worm is 0.63 units long.)

The whole loop, against the original

BAAIWorm's own code also runs here, outside the browser: its NEURON model and its SoftCon body, built without its CUDA viewer. Started the same way, the two runs give the same muscle signals to within 0.0001 at every step for 20 s (the range is 0 to 0.8). For the first 10 s, no vertex of the two bodies is more than 0.02 units apart (3% of the body length). Then the small differences in the brain grow, as they do between any two runs of a swimming body, and the paths separate.

The input

15 sensory neurons get a current clamp. Here it plays the 2 s pattern that BAAIWorm's readout was fit on. That is the pattern of the original's own demo. In the paper, this current comes from the change in odor at the head. That closed loop also runs here. With food placed 2 units away in 10 directions, the worm ended farther from the food after 60 s in all 10 runs. BAAIWorm's own code did the same in all 10, so this page does not use the closed loop.

Cutting the wiring

The sliders scale the strength of every chemical synapse or every gap junction. At 0% every connection of that kind is gone, as in the paper's experiments that removed them. Weaker connections in between are this page's addition. With all chemical synapses gone, the worm no longer swims forward and drifts slowly backward. With the gap junctions at half strength, it swims at about 1/20 of its normal speed. The readout was fit to the intact circuit, so this shows the effect of the cut. It does not show how a real worm adapts.

What is not real

  • BAAIWorm models only 136 of the 302 neurons. The others are not in this circuit.
  • The input current is large for the small sensory cells. Some of them, such as URYVL, go several hundred millivolts below rest. This is a property of BAAIWorm's model, and the page keeps it.
  • The body and water are BAAIWorm's. The drag grows with the square of the speed, as for a large, fast swimmer. A real worm is 1 mm long, and in water viscosity controls its motion.
  • Every neuron starts at −65 mV, as in BAAIWorm. In the first half second the brain settles and the head swings round. BAAIWorm's own run does the same.
  • The neurons are drawn by fitting the NeuroML coordinates into the mesh, so their place in the body is approximate.
