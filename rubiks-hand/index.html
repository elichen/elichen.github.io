Real 26-Cubelet Physics, Solved Live in Your Browser

Every move you watch above happens through genuine rigid-body contact physics inside MuJoCo compiled to WebAssembly. The Rubik's cube is not a rigid block with swapped textures: it is a 27-body articulated puzzle consisting of a free-floating core, 6 center hinges, and 20 edge and corner cubelets mounted on ball joints with beveled collision meshes. The cube itself has zero motors—only the 20 position actuators of the Shadow Dexterous Hand can push, cradle, flip, and twist its layers.

Full Cube State & Closed-Loop Manipulation

At 50 Hz, the control loop reads the complete physical state of both the Shadow Hand and the Rubik's cube: 24 hand joint angles and velocities, 20 actuator targets, all 5 fingertip positions relative to the cube, the cube's 3D pose and velocities, and all 6 internal face hinge angles and angular velocities. Rather than forcing a single policy to compromise between rotating the whole cube rigidly and clamping the bottom two layers while torqueing the top layer, solving is factored into two specialized PPO policies trained on Google Colab GPU with MuJoCo-Warp:

Reorient brings any requested face normal straight up (+Z) while keeping all six internal faces locked to multiples of 90° and squaring the cube's yaw with the palm. Twist starts from real terminal grasp states collected from Reorient and clamps the bottom two layers with the palm and fingers while rotating the top face by +90° or −90°. Both policies are iteratively co-trained with closed-loop DAgger state harvesting (real post-twist exits, bailout catches, double turns, and skewed-slice straightening) under Sim-to-WASM physics domain randomization so handovers stay tight across dozens of consecutive moves.

Live Kociemba Re-Planning

A pure-JavaScript 6-coset two-phase Kociemba solver decodes the exact 54-sticker configuration directly from the 20 physical cubelet quaternions in MuJoCo and searches all 3 rotational axes (forward and inverse) to pick solutions with the fewest cube flips and 180° turns. Because the controller verifies live face alignment after every move—invoking Twist directly to snap any skewed slice back to 90° before continuing—you can grab and perturb the cube with your mouse mid-solve and watch the hand recover and re-plan from whatever physical state the cube ended up in.
