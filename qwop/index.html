LEARNING TO QWOP

Keys pressed by the network. Press Q, W, O or P to take over.

Four keys drive a floppy runner's thighs and calves. A hand-scripted teacher can barely shuffle. A network cloned from it shuffles exactly as well, so exactly that DAgger found nothing to fix. Then reinforcement learning takes that shuffle and turns it into an 18.6-second hundred metres.

How it was trained

The physics is a small custom rigid-body solver written twice, once in Python for training and once in JavaScript for this page, and checked to agree to floating-point precision. Every network is a 77-input, two-layer tanh MLP that picks one of nine key combinations thirty times a second. The first 21-second gait trained on a laptop CPU in half an hour; a JAX port of the same simulator on a laptop GPU then ground out the last three seconds, mostly by turning exploration up.

Did the imitation stages matter? For reinforcement learning, yes: started from the cloned shuffle, PPO reached a 22-second hundred metres in about 125M steps, while the same PPO from random weights was still at 38 seconds and falling a quarter of the time. Forbidding the knee tap produces a tidy upright walk, at a third of the speed.

Stats under each brain are measured over 2,048 runs with slightly randomised starting poses. Code is in qwop/train.
